Seller guide

Selling your first home: a timeline

A practical week-by-week path from deciding to sell to handing over the keys — with the parts that genuinely change in Washington, Arizona and New York called out along the way.

Set expectations

Preparation plus a 30–45 day closing is common — not guaranteed

Many sellers spend three to four weeks preparing, then another 30–45 days from accepted offer to closing when the buyer is financing. Cash sales can move faster. Repairs, appraisal gaps, title problems, HOA documents and — in New York — co-op board review can add weeks.

Work backward from your move. If you must be out by a fixed date, choose your listing date first and leave a buffer for the buyer's loan and recording. A beautiful timeline with no slack is still a fragile timeline.
Week by week

From decision to keys

Weeks −4 to −3

Decide, price and choose your route

Review comparable sales, decide what work is worth doing, and run a Seller Net Sheet. Choose an agent, a hybrid service, or FSBO with a realistic plan for contracts, disclosures and deadlines.

Weeks −3 to −2

Fix what buyers will flag

Handle safety issues, leaks and obvious deferred maintenance. Declutter and deep-clean before attempting larger projects; most cosmetic renovations do not return their full cost on a short timeline.

Week −1

Paperwork, photos and launch prep

Complete required seller disclosures, gather HOA and utility information, write the listing, and schedule photography after the home is ready — not before.

Listing week

Launch and gather feedback

Make showings easy, track showing feedback, and watch the first week's traffic closely. Silence is market information; repeated comments about the same issue are a pattern.

Offer week

Compare more than price

Line up financing strength, earnest money, contingencies, requested credits, closing date and proof of funds beside the headline price. The cleanest offer is not always the highest — or the lowest.

Contract week 1

Open the file and inspect

The escrow/title company or attorneys open the transaction file. Expect the buyer's inspection, title search and appraisal order. Keep the home accessible and preserve it in the condition promised.

Contract week 2

Resolve requests and underwriting

Respond to inspection requests in writing, address title questions, and monitor the buyer's appraisal and loan conditions. Put every repair, credit and date change into a signed amendment.

Contract week 3

Clear the path to closing

Confirm clear-to-close status, review your settlement figures, schedule the buyer's final walkthrough, arrange utilities and plan the move. Ask early how and when proceeds will be disbursed.

Closing week

Sign, fund, record, hand over keys

Sign closing documents, confirm funding and recording, and deliver possession exactly as the contract states. Keep copies of the settlement statement and payoff confirmations for your tax records.

State differences

What changes in WA, AZ and NY

Washington — escrow-led

A neutral escrow or title company commonly holds funds and documents, coordinates signing, pays off liens, records the deed and disburses proceeds. An attorney is optional for most sellers, not the routine closing driver.

Arizona — escrow-led

Arizona also commonly uses a neutral escrow/title company to manage funds, documents and recording. Build in time for HOA resale documents and any contract-specific inspection response deadlines.

New York — attorney-customary

Buyers and sellers customarily hire their own real-estate attorneys; engage yours before the contract is final. Co-op sales add a board application and approval step that can materially extend the schedule.

The practical difference: in WA and AZ, ask early who your escrow officer is and what they need from you. In NY, ask who your attorney is before offers are negotiated — contract practice and closing roles are attorney-centered.
