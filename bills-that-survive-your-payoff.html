Homeowner guide

Bills that survive your payoff

The mortgage can disappear while property tax, insurance and HOA bills keep arriving. Use this printable checklist to take over the job escrow used to do — before the first bill surprises you.

The short version

Mortgage-free is not bill-free

With a typical escrowed mortgage, part of every monthly payment is held back for property taxes and homeowner's insurance. At payoff, that collection system stops. The underlying bills do not — only the person responsible for remembering and paying them changes.

Do this in the payoff month: confirm who will bill you directly, where each bill will be sent, the next due date, and whether any amount remains in escrow to be disbursed or refunded. Do not assume the final mortgage statement answers all four questions.
Printable checklist

The post-payoff checklist

Print this page and check each item off as you confirm the biller, amount, due date and payment method.

Property taxes

County or municipal taxes continue for as long as you own the home. Some owners receive one annual bill; others pay in installments, and supplemental bills can follow a reassessment or change in ownership records.

Write down: assessor/tax collector, parcel number, installment dates.

Homeowner's insurance

The lender will no longer require or pay the policy, but fire, storm and liability losses do not retire with the loan. A lapse can leave the home uninsured and may complicate a later policy.

Write down: renewal date, premium, insurer and payment address.

HOA or condo dues

Monthly, quarterly or annual dues continue under the community documents. Special assessments can arrive separately. In many places, unpaid association amounts can become a lien and, in serious cases, support foreclosure.

Write down: dues cycle, portal login, assessment contact.

Flood or special insurance

Flood, earthquake, wind or other separate policies may still be prudent — or required by an association — even when no lender is involved. The risk decision is now yours, not the servicer's.

Write down: each separate policy and renewal date.

Parcel, district & utility charges

Special-district assessments, sewer or water charges, and certain municipal utility bills may be billed with taxes or separately. Whether a charge can become a lien depends on the jurisdiction and provider.

Write down: every recurring charge that is tied to the property.

Maintenance reserve

A roof, furnace or plumbing failure is not a recurring government bill, but it is a predictable ownership cost. Add a monthly reserve so “free and clear” does not mean one repair away from debt.

Write down: monthly reserve and the account where it will live.
What changed?

How escrow worked

1. Collected monthly

Your mortgage payment bundled principal and interest with roughly one-twelfth of expected annual taxes and insurance.

2. Held for due dates

The servicer held the escrow portion and adjusted it after an annual analysis when taxes or premiums changed. A cushion may have been included where permitted.

3. Paid the biller

Escrow disbursed the tax and insurance payments when due. At payoff, the account is closed after final activity; watch for the final accounting and any refund.

HOA dues are often paid directly by owners even while a mortgage exists, but practices vary. Check your closing and servicer records rather than assuming escrow covered them.

Replace escrow

Self-escrow in three steps

01

Total the year

Add annual property tax, insurance, HOA dues and other recurring property bills. Use the latest actual bills, not last year's escrow estimate.

02

Divide by 12

Automatically move that amount every month — ideally on payday — into a separate savings account or clearly labeled sub-account used only for home bills.

03

Calendar & true-up

Set reminders 45 and 14 days before each due date. Once a year, compare the new tax and insurance bills with your set-aside and adjust before a shortage builds.
