How School Enrollment Works in Each Metro

The rules are genuinely different in each market — and in two of them, your address partly determines your options, so it pays to understand this before you pick a neighborhood.

Seattle Public Schools assigns every student to an attendance-area school based on home address. Eastside districts (Bellevue, Lake Washington, Issaquah, and others) work the same way — neighborhood schools by boundary.

The mechanics

  • Default assignment: your address determines your attendance-area school for elementary, middle, and high school. The district's address lookup tool confirms your assignment.
  • Open Enrollment for School Choice: each January, families can apply to attend a different school (for the 2026–27 cycle the window was January 5–31, with late applications through March 31). Admission is by space availability and tiebreakers — it is not guaranteed.
  • Option schools: Seattle also runs option schools (STEM, arts, language immersion, K–8 models) that families can request during choice season, subject to seat availability.
  • Mid-year movers: new-student enrollment stays open during the school year — you enroll at your attendance-area school when you arrive.

Seattle Public Schools — Enrollment  ·  School Choice

Arizona has the most flexible system of the three: state open-enrollment law plus one of the country's largest charter-school landscapes.

The mechanics

  • Open enrollment is the law (A.R.S. § 15-816): your child can apply to attend any public school in Arizona — even outside your home district — as long as the school has classroom space.
  • District schools still assign by boundary by default; open enrollment is how you transfer within or across districts.
  • Charter schools are tuition-free public schools, open to all students. By law they admit every timely applicant up to capacity, then use a lottery — no admissions tests allowed.
  • Practical note: with this much choice, most relocating families shortlist 3–5 schools and apply to several. Popular charters fill their lotteries early in the enrollment season.

Arizona Department of Education

NYC is a hybrid: zoned for elementary, choice-based for middle and high school — run through one citywide application system.

The mechanics

  • Elementary: most schools are zoned — zoned students get first priority for seats. You can rank up to 12 programs on the kindergarten application, including un-zoned "schools of choice."
  • Middle and high school: applied for through MySchools, the city's online system. Application windows typically run in the fall (roughly October–December); specialized high schools admit by the SHSAT exam.
  • New arrivals: families moving to NYC mid-year enroll year-round through a Family Welcome Center — you do not wait for an application window.
  • What this means for house hunting: for elementary grades, your address strongly shapes your options, so many families research zones before they commit to a neighborhood.

Moving Mid-Year? Read This First

Job relocations rarely line up with the school calendar. Here is how to land smoothly when you arrive between September and June.

1

Start With Your Assigned School

Your attendance-area or zoned school enrolls new residents during the school year. It is your guaranteed seat while you explore other options.

2

Bring the Paperwork

Birth certificate, immunization records, transcripts or report cards, proof of address — and IEP/504 paperwork if applicable, so services transfer without gaps.

3

Ask About Choice Windows

Arriving before a new school year? Ask each district about its school-choice application deadlines — Seattle's runs in January, NYC's in the fall, Arizona charters run lotteries on their own schedules.

4

Confirm Transportation

A seat at a choice, charter, or magnet school does not always come with a bus route. Ask about transportation before you commit — it affects where you can realistically live.

How to Research Safety for Any Address

We will not characterize any neighborhood's safety — but we will hand you the same official data the professionals use, and show you how to read it.

Start national: FBI Crime Data Explorer

The FBI's interactive tool for reported crime data at the national, state, and agency level — the baseline source everything else builds on. crime-data-explorer.fbi.gov

Then go local: your metro's police data

Local dashboards are more current and more granular than national data. Seattle publishes police data through the City of Seattle Open Data portal; Phoenix publishes crime statistics through the Phoenix Police Department; New York publishes precinct-level trends through NYPD CompStat.

Read trends, not snapshots

A single month of data tells you almost nothing. Pull the same precinct or beat over several years and look at the direction — and compare across the metro, not just within one city, so you have context.

See it at different times of day

Any area reads differently at 10am and 10pm. If you cannot visit in person, ask us to include an evening pass in your video walkthrough — you will see street lighting, foot traffic, and parking patterns for yourself.

Talk to the precinct

Local precincts and community-affairs officers answer resident questions routinely. It is the most direct primary source there is — and it costs nothing.

Things Relocators Ask Us Most

The practical details that surprise families moving between these three states.

School Calendars Differ

Many Phoenix-area districts run modified year-round calendars (shorter summer, longer breaks through the year), while Seattle and NYC run traditional calendars. Confirm the calendar with the specific district — it affects move timing and childcare planning.

Kindergarten Cutoffs Differ

Washington and Arizona typically require a child to be 5 by August 31; New York City uses December 31. A child who qualifies for kindergarten in one metro may not in another — verify with the district before you assume.

Bring Immunization Records

All three states require immunization records for school enrollment, but the required vaccines and exemption rules vary by state. Bring the full record and check the new state's requirements before your first enrollment visit.
