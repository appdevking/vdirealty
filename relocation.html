What Your Paycheck Looks Like in Each Metro

Same $200,000 salary, three very different take-home numbers. State and city income taxes are the swing factor — here's the rough math for a single filer in 2026.

Gross salary $200,000 $200,000 $200,000
Federal income tax (est.) $36,734 $36,734 $36,734
FICA — Social Security + Medicare (est.) $14,339 $14,339 $14,339
State + city income tax (est.) $0 (no state income tax) $4,791 (2.5% flat) $18,077 (state + NYC)
Estimated take-home $148,927 $144,136 $130,850
≈ per month $12,411 $12,011 $10,904

How the state lines are calculated: WA — no state income tax. AZ — 2.5% flat rate on Arizona taxable income ($200,000 minus the $8,350 single standard deduction = $191,650 × 2.5%). NY — state tax ≈ $10,760 on NY taxable income ($200,000 minus the $8,000 standard deduction) using 2026's reduced rates (3.90%–5.90%), plus NYC tax ≈ $7,317 (3.078%–3.876%) on the same base. NYC's city tax applies to residents of the five boroughs — commuters living in New Jersey, Westchester, or Long Island don't pay it.

The bottom line: at $200K, a Seattle paycheck keeps roughly $18,000 more per year than the same salary in New York City, and about $4,800 more than in Phoenix — before any difference in housing costs. That's money that goes straight into your home-buying budget.

Make the Most of Your Relocation Package

Most employers offer one of two structures — and more of it is negotiable than people realize. General guidance, not legal or tax advice.

Lump-Sum vs. Managed Moves

Lump sum: your employer gives you a fixed cash amount and you manage the move yourself — maximum flexibility, but you keep the receipts and the risk of overruns.

Managed: a relocation company handles movers, temporary housing, and logistics against company policy — less hassle, less choice.

Ask which one you're getting before you sign the offer — it changes your home-search timeline.

What's Often Negotiable

• One or two house-hunting trips (flights + hotel) before you move
• Temporary housing — typically 30–60 days; ask for 60 if you're buying sight-unseen
• Closing-cost coverage or a credit toward them
• Lease-break reimbursement on your current rental
• Spousal/partner job-search support
• Shipment and storage of household goods
• A sign-on bonus earmarked to offset moving costs

Questions to Ask HR

• Is any part of the package tax-grossed-up, or do I owe tax on it?
• Is there a repayment (clawback) clause if I leave within 12–24 months?
• What's the deadline to use each benefit?
• Does the policy cover home-sale assistance if I own now?
• Can unused benefits convert to cash?
• Who is my single point of contact once I accept?

Relocation benefits vary widely by employer and are subject to company policy and tax law. Get every commitment in writing and consult a tax professional about the tax treatment of your package. This is general information, not legal or tax advice.

First 30 Days: The Landing Checklist

The paperwork clock starts the day you become a resident. Deadlines differ by state — here's what each one requires.

Washington

• Driver's license: transfer within 30 days of becoming a resident.
• Vehicle registration: register within 30 days of becoming a resident.
• No state income tax return to file — but update your address with your employer for correct withholding.
• Register to vote — Washington votes entirely by mail; ballots arrive automatically once registered.

Arizona

• Driver's license & registration: Arizona moves fast — state law requires a driver's license immediately once residency criteria are met (working in AZ, registering to vote, enrolling kids in school, etc.), and vehicle registration within 15 days of establishing residency.
• No written or road test if you hold a valid out-of-state license — vision screening only.
• Vehicles in the Phoenix metro area need an emissions test before registration.
• File a part-year resident return for your move year.

New York

• Driver's license: exchange your out-of-state license within 30 days of becoming a resident — no road test if your current license is valid.
• Vehicle registration: register within 30 days; New York requires NY-based insurance on any NY-registered vehicle — out-of-state policies don't count.
• If you live in the five boroughs, expect NYC income tax on top of state tax (see the paycheck section above).
• File a part-year resident return for your move year.

Set Up the Household

Electric, gas, water, trash, and internet — schedule connections a week before move-in so nothing's dark on night one. Update renter's or homeowner's insurance to the new address.

Move Your Address

USPS change of address, then banks, credit cards, employer payroll, insurance, voter registration, subscriptions, and anyone who sends you anything important. Do it in one sitting — it takes 30 minutes.

Land Softly

Find a primary-care doctor, dentist, and vet before you need one. Locate the nearest urgent care, pharmacy, and grocery run. Then introduce yourself to a neighbor — local knowledge beats search results.

Deadlines and requirements change — verify current rules at dol.wa.gov (Washington), azdot.gov (Arizona), and dmv.ny.gov (New York) before you act. The Arizona "as soon as residency criteria are met" requirement reflects ADOT new-resident guidance; confirm timing for your situation directly with ADOT.
