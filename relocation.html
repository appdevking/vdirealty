Pick Your Metro

Neighborhood snapshots for the three metros we serve. Price ranges are rough estimates for orientation only — not appraisals.

Tech workers heading to Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta, and Boeing. The Eastside (Bellevue, Redmond, Kirkland) pairs top-rated schools with short commutes to the region's biggest campuses.

Bellevue

Downtown high-rises, established neighborhoods, and highly rated public schools. Short commute to Microsoft's Redmond campus and downtown Seattle.

Typical range: ~$900K – $2M+

Redmond

Home of Microsoft's main campus. Mix of townhomes and single-family neighborhoods with trail access along the Sammamish River.

Typical range: ~$800K – $1.6M

Kirkland

Waterfront downtown, Google's Kirkland campus, and walkable shops and restaurants along Lake Washington.

Typical range: ~$850K – $1.8M

Issaquah & Sammamish

Newer construction, highly rated schools, and trail access at the foot of the Cascades. Popular with buyers wanting more space.

Typical range: ~$900K – $1.7M

Seattle — North End

Ballard, Fremont, and Green Lake offer walkable urban villages with character homes. Good fit for Amazon and South Lake Union commuters.

Typical range: ~$750K – $1.5M

Renton & Bothell

More home for the budget with growing downtowns. Renton sits near Boeing and Sea-Tac; Bothell feeds the I-405 tech corridor.

Typical range: ~$600K – $1.1M

Semiconductor, tech, healthcare, and finance workers heading to Intel, TSMC, Honeywell, American Express, and Banner Health. The Valley offers newer homes, master-planned communities, and no state income-tax shock for many movers.

Scottsdale

Upscale dining, golf, and Old Town nightlife. Strong draw for executives and buyers wanting a polished, amenity-rich base.

Typical range: ~$650K – $1.5M+

Chandler

Intel's Chandler campus anchors this family-oriented East Valley hub with highly rated schools and master-planned neighborhoods.

Typical range: ~$550K – $1M

Gilbert

One of the Valley's most requested suburbs — newer builds, community events, and a strong school reputation.

Typical range: ~$550K – $950K

Tempe

Home to Arizona State University. Lively, central, and well-connected by light rail — a fit for younger professionals.

Typical range: ~$450K – $800K

Phoenix — Arcadia & Desert Ridge

Arcadia offers character homes near Camelback Mountain; Desert Ridge brings newer master-planned living in North Phoenix near TSMC.

Typical range: ~$600K – $1.4M

Peoria & North Glendale

Value picks on the northwest side with newer construction and easy freeway access to the Loop 303 employment corridor.

Typical range: ~$450K – $750K

Finance, tech, media, and healthcare professionals heading to Manhattan, Brooklyn, and the surrounding suburbs. NYC moves hinge on commute math — subway lines, PATH, and Metro-North shape every shortlist.

Manhattan

Condos and co-ops near the Financial District, Midtown, and Hudson Yards. Walk-to-work living at a premium — co-op boards add steps to the process.

Typical range: ~$1M – $3M+

Brooklyn

Park Slope, Williamsburg, and Brooklyn Heights blend brownstones, new condos, and strong subway access to Manhattan offices.

Typical range: ~$900K – $2.5M

Queens

Astoria and Forest Hills offer relative value with quick Midtown commutes. Diverse neighborhoods with deep transit options.

Typical range: ~$650K – $1.4M

Hoboken & Jersey City

PATH-train access to Manhattan, waterfront views, and newer high-rises. Popular with finance commuters wanting more space per dollar.

Typical range: ~$750K – $1.8M

Westchester

Scarsdale, White Plains, and Yonkers offer suburban living on Metro-North lines into Grand Central. A classic family relocation corridor.

Typical range: ~$700K – $1.8M

Long Island — Nassau

Great Neck, Manhasset, and Garden City pair LIRR commutes with highly regarded schools. Single-family homes dominate.

Typical range: ~$800K – $2M
